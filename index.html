Northern Region: a 90-day strategic execution plan to deepen cold-chain market penetration across six states

SituationThe business operated mainly from Lagos and served the North project by project, even though Northern Nigeria is a major source of agricultural produce and cold-storage demand. Awareness and coverage there were low.
My roleRegional Manager, Northern Region, reporting to the MD/CEO. I wrote and submitted the formal First 90-Day Strategic Execution Plan and lead its delivery from the Abuja regional office: myself, three business development officers and a technician, backed by headquarters project teams.
FocusFCT, Kano, Kaduna, Sokoto, Kogi and Kwara, across cold rooms, blast freezers, ice block machines and maintenance services.
Days 1 to 30
Review and diagnose
Project mobilisation, business analysis and data collection, competitor and leading-practice mapping across the focus states, opportunity and capability mapping, and a current-versus-future gap register.
Days 31 to 60
Develop and activate
Mid-point alignment with executive management, strategic trade-offs and market prioritisation, and financial and risk analysis for each option.
Days 61 to 90
Execute, validate and recommend
Opportunity rationalisation, then a recommended regional strategy and roadmap: priority states and sectors, strategic accounts, resources, owners and timelines.
Current status: in progress, and I am leading it now. The plan runs as a ten-step structure with a defined deliverable at each step. The 2026 company plan sets the Northern targets: presence in Kano and Sokoto plus 8 installations. Measured outcomes will be added here as they land.
Evidence: First 90-Day Strategic Execution Plan, Northern Region. 2026 Strategic Focus (Northern market penetration KPI).
Strategy to execution

From a gap analysis to a three-year roadmap and annual plans that get run

SituationInformation from departments was not reaching management meetings, and there was no shared structure for turning priorities into owned, tracked actions.
My roleLed as internal strategy consultant to the MD and executive management.
ApproachCurrent-state gap analysis, operating model design and a three-year roadmap, then annual plans built on OKRs. 2023 focused on business diagnosis, needs identification, future-state definition, risk assessment and change recommendations, which led to product improvements through the Gap Model of Quality. 2024 closed visibility and customer experience gaps enterprise-wide by establishing a digital marketing unit and a dedicated customer experience unit. The 2025 plan has nine pillars with owners, deadlines and KPIs. The 2026 plan starts from an honest review of what slowed 2025, mainly accountability gaps, and adds a monthly Strategic Execution Accountability Forum alongside weekly and quarterly reviews.
Turnover tripled in two years, and monthly and quarterly business reviews run on a performance tracking system.
Evidence: 2025 Strategic Plan, 2026 Strategic Focus, Gap Analysis and Strategic Plan workbook.
Digital transformation

An enterprise-wide ERP rollout, led as a people change as much as a system change

SituationAn ERP that was only partly used, with manual reporting and inconsistent adherence to approved processes.
My roleLed the transformation and managed stakeholders across functions.
ApproachADKAR change model with a phased rollout. Usage targets went into the strategic plan itself: recruitment, appraisals, maintenance scheduling, asset registers and management reports all moved onto the ERP.
100% compliance by Q1 2025 and 40% faster procurement-to-invoicing. The 2026 target is 85% or more process adoption. Outcome: transparent financial management and reporting.
Evidence: ERP pillars in the 2025 and 2026 strategic plans. Tools: ERPNext.
Decision support

AI-enabled dashboards that put real numbers in front of management

SituationReporting was informal and data was weak, so decisions leaned on opinion.
My roleIdentified the decision-making gap, proposed AI integration with the ERP, and worked with developers to deliver it.
ApproachSpecified seven ERPNext BI dashboards (finance, marketing, HR, CRM, projects, executive and strategy), each with its KPI, source module, frequency and chart type. Standardised an 11-section executive report with one rule: figures come from the ERP or CRM, never estimates, and any KPI under 85% needs a variance explanation.
AI dashboards live for executive, finance, sales and marketing, and strategy functions, feeding weekly reviews.
Evidence: ERPNext BI Dashboard Specification, Head of Marketing Executive Performance Report.
Customer experience and product quality

Closing the gap between what customers expect and what they receive

SituationThe 2023 analysis found about half of customers satisfied, no customer service centre or showroom, and the MD personally handling external enquiries.
My roleDiagnosed the gaps, presented them to management, and led the fix.
ApproachUsed the expectations-versus-perceptions gap model in a March 2023 management presentation. Set up a dedicated Customer Service Office working in the ERP's CRM, defined product quality specifications, and planned after-sales: a 24/7 helpline, preventive maintenance packages, and repair-time and SLA tracking.
Customer satisfaction moved from below 50% to over 70%.
Evidence: Quality Gaps presentation (2023), Gap Analysis workbook.
Commercial growth

Building a lead engine, and structuring the next market-entry decision

SituationCoverage outside Lagos was thin, awareness of the brand was low in several regions, and expansion decisions needed a structured case.
My roleLed the marketing, business development and CX teams, and owned the market-entry analysis.

1,200+ leads a month and 10%+ lead-to-sale conversion.
Evidence: Market Expansion pillars in the 2025 and 2026 strategic plans, Marketing reporting template.
